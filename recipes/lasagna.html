Description

When most people outside of Italy think of lasagna, they think of ricotta cheese and mozzarella. However, a true traditional Northern Italian Lasagna (Lasagna alla Bolognese) relies on a rich, slow-simmered meat ragù, a creamy homemade béchamel sauce (besciamella), and plenty of Parmigiano-Reggiano. There is absolutely no ricotta or mozzarella in an authentic recipe from Emilia-Romagna.This recipe is a labor of love, but the results are unforgettable.

Ingredients

For the Traditional Ragù (Bolognese)

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1 medium yellow onion, very finely chopped
  • 1 large carrot, very finely chopped
  • 1 stalk celery, very finely chopped
  • 1 lb (450g) high-quality ground beef (chuck is best)
  • 1/2 lb (225g) ground pork (or finely minced pancetta)
  • 1 cup dry white wine (or dry red wine)
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1 cup beef broth (low sodium)
  • 1 ½ cups (350g) tomato purée (passata) or high-quality crushed tomatoes
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper

For the Besciamella (Béchamel Sauce)

  • 5 tbsp (70g) unsalted butter
  • ½ cup (60g) all-purpose flour
  • 4 cups (1 liter) whole milk, warmed
  • ¼ tsp freshly grated nutmeg (essential for authentic flavor)
  • Salt and a pinch of white pepper

For the Assembly

  • 1 lb (450g) lasagna sheets (fresh egg pasta is best, or high-quality dried durum wheat)
  • 2 ½ cups freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese (Do not use pre-shredded bagged cheese)
  • 1 tbsp butter (for greasing the baking dish)
  • Optional but highly traditional: Use Lasagna Verde (green spinach pasta sheets) for a truly authentic experience.

Instructions

Step 1: Make the Ragù (Can be done a day ahead)

  1. Soffritto: In a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven, heat the olive oil and butter over medium heat. Add the finely chopped onion, carrot, and celery. Sauté gently for about 8–10 minutes until the vegetables are very soft but not browned.
  2. Brown the Meat: Add the ground beef and pork. Break it up with a wooden spoon and cook until nicely browned (about 8 minutes).
  3. Deglaze: Pour in the wine and stir, scraping up any browned bits from the bottom of the pot. Let the wine simmer and evaporate completely.
  4. The Milk Step: Lower the heat and pour in the milk. Simmer gently until the milk has completely evaporated into the meat. (This traditional step protects the meat from the acidity of the tomatoes and makes it incredibly tender).
  5. Simmer: Stir in the tomato purée and beef broth. Season with a little salt and pepper. Turn the heat down to the absolute lowest setting. Cover slightly and let it simmer for 2 to 3 hours, stirring occasionally. If it gets too dry, add a splash of water or broth. The final sauce should be thick and rich, not watery. Taste and adjust seasoning at the end.

Step 2: Make the Besciamella

  1. In a medium saucepan, melt the butter over medium-low heat.
  2. Whisk in the flour all at once to create a roux. Cook for 1–2 minutes until it smells slightly toasted, but do not let it turn brown.
  3. Gradually pour in the warm milk, whisking continuously and vigorously to prevent lumps.
  4. Keep whisking until the sauce thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon (about 5–8 minutes).
  5. Remove from heat. Stir in the salt, white pepper, and freshly grated nutmeg. Press a piece of plastic wrap directly onto the surface of the sauce to prevent a skin from forming while you assemble.

Step 3: Prepare the Pasta

  1. If using fresh pasta, you can usually assemble it raw (check the package instructions).
  2. If using dried pasta sheets, bring a massive pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the sheets for 1–2 minutes (they should be very al dente). Transfer them immediately to a bowl of ice water to stop the cooking, then lay them flat on clean kitchen towels to dry.

Step 4: Assemble the Lasagna

  1. Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Grease your 9x13 inch baking dish with the 1 tbsp of butter.
  2. Base Layer: Spread a thin layer of Ragù and a few spoonfuls of Besciamella on the bottom of the dish.
  3. Layering:
    1. Lay down a layer of pasta sheets (overlap them very slightly).
    2. Spread a generous layer of Ragù over the pasta.
    3. Drizzle a layer of Besciamella over the meat.
    4. Sprinkle a handful of Parmigiano-Reggiano.
  4. Repeat: Repeat this process (Pasta -> Ragù -> Besciamella -> Cheese) for 4 to 5 layers.
  5. Top Layer: Finish with a final layer of pasta, completely cover it with the remaining Besciamella (so no dry pasta is exposed), dot with any remaining Ragù, and bury it in a thick blanket of the remaining Parmigiano-Reggiano.

Step 5: Bake and Rest

  1. Bake uncovered for 25–30 minutes until the sauce is bubbling.
  2. If the top isn't browned enough, turn on the broiler for the last 2–3 minutes (watch it closely so it doesn't burn).
  3. CRUCIAL STEP: Remove the lasagna from the oven and let it rest for at least 30 minutes before cutting. If you cut it immediately, the layers will slide apart and it will be a soupy mess. Resting allows the sauces to set, giving you those beautiful, distinct, structural layers when you pull a slice out.

Pro-Tips for Success:

  • Make Ahead:

    Ragù actually tastes better the next day. You can make the meat sauce and béchamel on Saturday, assemble the lasagna on Sunday morning, keep it in the fridge, and bake it for dinner. (Just add 15 minutes to the baking time if baking straight from the fridge).

  • Make Ahead:

    Unbaked, assembled lasagna freezes beautifully for up to 3 months. Thaw in the fridge overnight before baking.

  • Cheese:

    Buy a block of real Parmigiano-Reggiano and grate it yourself. The pre-shredded stuff is coated in cellulose (wood pulp) which prevents it from melting smoothly into the sauce. Serve with a wedge at the table!
